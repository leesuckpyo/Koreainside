How to Pay in Korea

Foreign-issued cards cover much of a trip to Korea, but a card that works at a hotel or chain can still fail at a small shop, online checkout or transit top-up. A little cash and a separate transit balance cover the gaps without turning payment into a complicated plan.

Where foreign cards can still get difficult

A successful payment at one business does not guarantee that the same card will work at the next. The merchant terminal, card network, issuer settings and the way a payment is authenticated can all change the result.

Acceptance changes between businesses and terminals

Major international cards such as Visa, Mastercard, JCB, Amex and UnionPay are accepted at many Korean stores, but acceptance still varies by merchant and terminal. Contactless support is not universal, while markets, street stalls and some very small businesses may still be easier with cash.

Online checkout may ask for local credentials

An overseas card that works in person may still fail on a Korean website when checkout requires local authentication, Korean phone verification or a domestic payment flow.

Mobile wallets have their own setup rules

Apple Pay is supported at participating NFC merchants, although the card in your wallet and the merchant terminal still need to be compatible. Korean services such as Kakao Pay and Naver Pay can require local identity, phone or payment setup, so they are not dependable defaults for a short visit. Other mobile wallets depend on the card, device and service setup.

Transit money may sit in a separate balance

T-money recharge can follow a different payment flow from an ordinary shop purchase. On multi-function visitor cards, the transportation balance and prepaid payment balance may also be separate.

Taxi card payments can still be interrupted

Many taxis accept cards or T-money, but terminal issues, card type and local conditions can still create friction.

Cash remains useful at the smallest businesses

Markets, street stalls and some small local businesses may not follow the same card pattern as hotels and chains. A small amount of cash covers those moments without requiring cash for the whole trip.

Getting cash in Korea

ATMs that accept overseas cards are available at airports, banks and some convenience-store or station locations, but support varies by machine.

The final cost of withdrawing or exchanging cash depends on the ATM operator, your card issuer, network fees and the exchange rate.

T-money and WOWPASS solve different parts of the trip

T-money keeps transit separate

T-money covers subway, bus and many taxi payments. It is not a general payment card for shopping, restaurants or online reservations.

Tipping & splitting bills

Tipping is not expected or customary in Korea. Service charges are rarely added. Splitting bills is common, and many restaurants accept separate card payments. Say "ttaro gyesanhae juseyo" to ask to pay separately.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my foreign credit card in Korea?

Yes. Foreign Visa and Mastercard cards work in many places, but acceptance can vary at small shops, online services and some terminals.

Do I need cash in Korea?

Yes, a small amount is useful for traditional markets, small vendors, backup payments and some transportation card recharge situations.

Can I use Apple Pay or Google Pay in Korea?

Sometimes. It depends on your card, wallet setup and the merchant terminal. Do not rely on it as your only payment method.

Is T-money a payment card?

T-money is mainly for transportation. It can work in some taxis and selected convenience store situations, but it is not a general shopping card.

Is WOWPASS better than T-money?

WOWPASS can be better if you want prepaid payments and currency exchange. T-money is simpler if you only need subway and bus travel.

Can I pay for taxis by card?

Many taxis accept cards, but keep a backup payment method because card type, terminal issues or local conditions can vary.

Why does my card fail online?

Some Korean online services require Korean identity verification, a Korean phone number or domestic payment authentication.

Do Korean payment apps work for tourists?

Some features may be limited because full setup can require Korean identity verification, Korean phone service or a Korean bank account.
