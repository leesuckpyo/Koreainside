Does Apple Pay Work in Korea for Tourists?

Yes. If you already use Apple Pay at home, you can use it at participating stores in Korea when the merchant supports contactless payment and the card network in your Wallet. You do not need to open a Korean bank account just to tap your existing overseas Apple Pay card at a compatible shop. Apple also confirms that Apple Pay can be used abroad in countries and regions that support contactless payments.

Public transport is where the story changes. In Korea, tapping an ordinary credit card from Apple Wallet at a subway gate is not the same thing as paying at a café. For buses and subways, Apple Wallet now supports a separate prepaid T-money transit card.

Once those two uses are separated, Apple Pay in Korea becomes much less confusing.

Your overseas Apple Pay card can work in Korean stores

A visitor does not need a Korean-issued card simply because the payment is happening in Korea.

If a card is already supported by Apple Pay through your bank at home, it can be used abroad where the merchant's terminal has NFC contactless payment enabled and the merchant also accepts that card's payment network. Apple specifically notes that both conditions matter.

That is why the same iPhone may work perfectly at one Korean shop and fail at the next.

The problem is not necessarily Apple Pay itself. A store may have an older terminal, contactless may not be enabled, or the merchant may not accept the network behind the card in your Wallet. Apple even warns that a contactless symbol on a reader does not guarantee that contactless payment is actually enabled there.

So Apple Pay is useful in Korea, but it is still worth carrying another way to pay.

You do not need a Korean Hyundai Card just to pay as a visitor

This is one of the easiest Apple Pay details to misunderstand.

Apple's current Korean list shows Hyundai Card as the participating issuer for cards issued in South Korea. That list matters when someone wants to add a Korean-issued credit or debit card to Apple Pay.

It does not mean every foreign visitor must obtain a Hyundai Card before using Apple Pay in Korea.

Apple separately states that Apple Pay works abroad in countries and regions that support contactless payments. A traveler using an eligible card already issued and added to Apple Wallet in another country is dealing with overseas card use, not with opening a Korean Apple Pay account.

For an American visitor, the useful question is therefore not “Do I have a Hyundai Card?” It is “Does this Korean terminal accept Apple Pay and the network behind the card already in my Wallet?”

A failed tap does not always mean your card was declined

Apple Pay can fail before the transaction becomes a normal issuer decline.

If nothing happens when you hold the phone near the reader, the terminal may not have contactless enabled or may not support the payment network attached to the card. That is different from the transaction reaching your bank and being rejected there.

If you also carry the physical card, inserting the chip may still work at a store where contactless does not.

And if the card itself begins failing at several staffed businesses, that is no longer just an Apple Pay question. The Card Declined in Korea guide is the better place to separate a terminal problem from an issuer-side decline.

Subway and bus travel use T-money in Apple Wallet

Korea does not treat ordinary Apple Pay card payments and public-transport payments as the same thing.

Apple and Tmoney launched prepaid T-money in Apple Wallet in July 2025. On a supported iPhone or Apple Watch, the T-money card can be used on Korean public transport with Express Mode, so the device does not need to be unlocked before every tap.

That means a visitor may have two quite different things inside the same Wallet:

  • a Visa, Mastercard or another payment card for shops; and
  • a prepaid T-money card for buses and subways.

Your foreign credit card does not simply become the transit card.

That distinction matters at the subway gate. If you want to use your iPhone or Apple Watch for Korean public transport, the relevant card is the T-money card stored in Apple Wallet.

The difficult part used to be topping up T-money

When Apple Wallet T-money launched in 2025, there was an important limitation for tourists.

Apple's launch documentation states that adding money to T-money directly inside Apple Wallet requires a credit or debit card issued in South Korea.

That created an awkward situation: tourists could add T-money to an iPhone, but many of them could not easily fund it with the overseas cards they already carried.

That old limitation is still important because the direct Wallet top-up route and the MobileTmoney app route are not the same thing.

MobileTmoney now gives international visitors another route

This is the part many older Korea travel guides miss.

The current MobileTmoney App Store listing says the app is available for international users, does not require sign-up for basic use, and allows top-up through Apple Pay. Its current release notes list Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay and JCB for that foreign-user top-up route.

So a visitor who cannot fund T-money directly inside Apple Wallet with an overseas card may still be able to top it up through MobileTmoney.

There is an important catch for American travelers: Visa is not currently listed among the supported foreign-card networks for MobileTmoney top-up. Tmoney's own developer response in the App Store has also stated that Visa is not supported for this foreign-user charging route.

That makes the card in your Wallet matter more than the Apple Pay logo alone.

If your only overseas card is Visa, do not assume that the tourist MobileTmoney top-up route will work for you just because the card works for ordinary Apple Pay purchases.

Apple Wallet top-up and MobileTmoney top-up are not the same thing

This distinction deserves to be stated plainly.

Inside Apple Wallet: Apple's published T-money terms say that direct top-up requires a South Korea-issued credit or debit card.

Inside MobileTmoney for international users: the current app supports Apple Pay top-up with Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay and JCB, without requiring the visitor to sign up first.

Both routes end with money on a T-money card in Apple Wallet, but the funding rules are different.

This is why two travelers can have the same iPhone and the same T-money card on screen but very different setup experiences depending on which payment card they brought to Korea.

iPhone and Apple Watch do not share one T-money card at the same time

If you plan to use an Apple Watch for the subway, there is another small detail worth knowing.

Apple says one T-money card can only be stored on one device at a time. A card can be moved between an iPhone and Apple Watch, but the same card cannot be active on both simultaneously. If you want a T-money card available on both devices at the same time, each device needs its own card.

For most tourists, that is not a problem. It simply means deciding which device you actually want to tap at the gate.

Express Mode is available on supported devices, so once T-money is set up, everyday transit use is much simpler than the setup process makes it sound.

A physical card is still useful when Apple Pay is not

There is no need to turn an iPhone into the only way you can pay during a trip.

Apple Pay acceptance still depends on the merchant terminal and card network, and the foreign-user MobileTmoney route currently does not support every card network.

Keeping a physical payment card with you gives you another option when contactless is unavailable.

And if setting up mobile T-money becomes more trouble than it is worth for your particular cards, using a physical T-money card is still a perfectly reasonable way to handle public transport.

The useful goal is not to make every payment happen through the iPhone. It is to know which parts of the trip Apple Pay can genuinely make easier.

Frequently asked questions

Does Apple Pay work in Korea?

Yes. Apple Pay works at participating Korean merchants with compatible NFC contactless terminals, provided the merchant also accepts the payment network behind your card. It is not available at every card terminal.

Can I use my US Apple Pay card in Korea?

Yes, if the card is already supported by Apple Pay through your issuer and is enabled for overseas use. Apple says Apple Pay can be used abroad in countries and regions that support contactless payments. Merchant and network compatibility still matter.

Do tourists need a Hyundai Card to use Apple Pay in Korea?

No. Hyundai Card is currently the participating issuer for supported cards issued in South Korea. A visitor using an eligible overseas card already in Apple Wallet does not need to obtain a Korean Hyundai Card simply to use Apple Pay at a compatible Korean merchant.

Can I use Apple Pay directly on the Seoul subway?

For public transport, Apple Wallet uses a prepaid T-money transit card. Add T-money to Wallet and use that transit card at supported buses and subway gates rather than assuming your ordinary credit card in Apple Pay works as the transit card.

Can foreign tourists top up Apple Wallet T-money?

Yes, but the method matters. Direct top-up inside Apple Wallet is documented by Apple as requiring a South Korea-issued card. MobileTmoney now provides an international-user route that supports Apple Pay top-up with Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay and JCB.

Can I top up MobileTmoney with a foreign Visa card?

Visa is not currently listed among the supported foreign-card networks in MobileTmoney's international-user top-up information. The current listed networks are Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay and JCB.

Can I use the same T-money card on my iPhone and Apple Watch?

Not at the same time. Apple says one T-money card can be on only one device at a time. You can move it between devices, or add separate T-money cards if you want one on each device.

Should I rely only on Apple Pay during a Korea trip?

It is better to have another payment method available. Apple Pay requires compatible merchant terminals and card networks, and MobileTmoney's international top-up route does not currently support every foreign card network.
