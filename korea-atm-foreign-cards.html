Using ATMs in Korea with a Foreign Card

Yes, you can withdraw Korean won with many foreign cards in Korea. The catch is that not every ATM handles every overseas card, even when the machine belongs to a familiar bank.

The easiest place to start is the logos on the ATM. Visa tells travelers to look for the PLUS mark, while Mastercard directs its cardholders to machines showing Mastercard, Maestro or Cirrus. Hana Bank also identifies foreign-card networks supported by its Global ATMs, including Visa/PLUS, Mastercard/Maestro/Cirrus, JCB and UnionPay.

Find an ATM that is actually set up for foreign cards

A bank logo on the wall is not enough by itself. What matters is whether that particular machine connects to the network on your card.

“Global ATM” is a useful sign to look for in Korea, and some banks use that label for machines intended to work with international card networks. Still, the safest habit is to look at the actual network marks on the ATM rather than assuming every machine from the same bank works the same way. Hana Bank, for example, explicitly lists several international networks for its Global ATMs.

Visa cards with PLUS access should look for the PLUS mark. Mastercard, Maestro and Cirrus cardholders can use ATMs carrying the corresponding marks. Your own card still has to be enabled for overseas cash withdrawals.

A card that works in shops can still fail at an ATM

Paying for dinner and withdrawing cash are not the same transaction.

Your card issuer can allow overseas purchases while applying different rules to ATM withdrawals. PIN requirements, withdrawal limits and account restrictions also come from the institution that issued the card. Mastercard specifically directs cardholders back to their issuer for account withdrawal limits, restrictions and PIN questions.

This is why an ATM rejection does not automatically mean your card has stopped working in Korea. If it still works at shops and restaurants, treat the ATM problem separately.

If you are using a credit card rather than a debit card, also remember that a cash withdrawal can fall under your issuer's cash-advance terms. The fees and rules come from the card agreement, not from one Korea-wide policy.

If one ATM says no, try another machine

One failed ATM is not a useful verdict on the whole card.

The machine may not support the right network, the transaction may exceed a limit, or the issuer may not approve that particular withdrawal. Another ATM—especially one clearly showing your network mark—can behave differently.

There is little value in entering the same card into the same machine again and again if nothing changes. Try another suitable ATM first. If several compatible machines reject the card, then it becomes more useful to check the issuer app or contact the bank that issued the card.

Mastercard's own ATM guidance makes the same distinction: ATM availability and account restrictions can vary, and the issuing financial institution is the place to confirm personal limits and restrictions.

At Incheon Airport, you have more than one chance

You do not have to make the first ATM you see after landing work.

Incheon International Airport currently lists ATMs from KB Bank, Woori Bank and Hana Bank across both terminals, along with common-use ATMs. Some airport ATM facilities operate around the clock, while others have set operating hours.

That makes the airport a convenient place to try an initial withdrawal, but the same rule still applies: check the machine and card-network support rather than assuming every airport ATM accepts every overseas card.

There is also no need to withdraw your entire trip budget just because you have found a machine that works. Korea is highly card-friendly, and cash is mainly useful for the situations where cards or other payment methods are less convenient.

ATM fees can come from more than one place

The number shown by the Korean ATM is not necessarily the whole cost of the withdrawal.

An ATM owner can charge a fee, and your own card issuer may also charge for international ATM use, foreign exchange or a cash advance. Mastercard says ATM-owner fees should be disclosed when the withdrawal begins, while the issuer's own fee policy has to be checked separately with the card provider.

Visa likewise notes that overseas ATM withdrawals can involve ATM charges as well as fees connected to the card account and foreign exchange.

There is no single “Korean ATM fee” that applies to everyone. Two travelers using the same machine can end up with different total costs because their cards have different terms.

When the ATM offers KRW or your home currency

A Korean ATM may offer to convert the withdrawal into your card's home currency. That is Dynamic Currency Conversion, usually shortened to DCC.

If DCC is offered, Visa requires the screen to show both currencies, the exchange rate and any additional fee or markup, and the choice must remain with the cardholder.

Seeing a familiar dollar, euro or other home-currency amount can feel reassuring, but familiarity does not tell you whether the conversion is cheaper. The alternative is to leave the withdrawal in Korean won and let your card issuer apply its own foreign-exchange terms.

Read what the ATM is actually offering before you confirm. The useful comparison is between the conversion shown on the screen and the foreign-exchange terms attached to your own card—not a blanket rule that every traveler must make the same choice.

Withdrawing cash without turning it into a project

Once you find a suitable ATM, the process is usually straightforward.

  1. Look for the network on your card. A Visa/PLUS or Mastercard/Maestro/Cirrus mark is more useful than simply recognizing the bank name.
  2. Select English and choose a cash withdrawal. Have the card's PIN ready; the issuer controls PIN and account rules.
  3. Read the amount and any ATM fee before confirming. If the machine shows a fee you do not want to pay, you can cancel and try another ATM.
  4. If a currency-conversion screen appears, read both options. Look at the exchange rate and any markup before accepting or declining DCC.
  5. If the withdrawal fails, change the machine before changing everything else. A different compatible ATM can tell you more than repeatedly retrying the same one.
  6. If several suitable ATMs reject the card, check with the issuer. Your bank can confirm whether overseas cash withdrawals, account limits or card restrictions are getting in the way.

You do not need to know the entire Korean banking system to get cash. You only need a machine that speaks to your card network and an issuer that allows the withdrawal.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a foreign card at an ATM in Korea?

Yes, if the ATM supports the network on your card and your issuer allows overseas cash withdrawals. Look for network marks such as Visa/PLUS or Mastercard, Maestro and Cirrus rather than assuming every ATM will accept the card.

What does “Global ATM” mean in Korea?

Banks may use the term for ATMs that support international card networks. Hana Bank, for example, lists Mastercard/Maestro/Cirrus, Visa/PLUS, JCB and UnionPay for foreign cards at its Global ATMs. The label is useful, but the network marks on the individual machine still matter.

Why does my card work in stores but not at an ATM?

Purchases and cash withdrawals follow different account rules. Your issuer can place separate limits or restrictions on ATM withdrawals even when ordinary card purchases work normally.

What should I do if a Korean ATM rejects my card?

Try another ATM that clearly supports your card network. If several compatible ATMs reject the card, check your issuer's app or contact the issuer about overseas cash withdrawals, limits and account restrictions.

Do I need a PIN to withdraw cash in Korea?

You should have the PIN associated with the card available. Mastercard's ATM guidance instructs cardholders to enter their PIN when withdrawing cash and says PIN questions should be handled by the issuing financial institution.

How much does it cost to withdraw cash from a Korean ATM?

There is no single fee that applies to every traveler. The ATM owner may charge a fee, and your card issuer may add its own ATM, foreign-exchange or cash-advance charges.

Should I choose KRW or my home currency at the ATM?

If the ATM offers DCC, read the exchange rate and any additional fees or markup shown on the screen. Your other option is to keep the transaction in Korean won and use your card issuer's currency-conversion terms. Compare the actual terms rather than choosing only because one currency looks more familiar.

Can I withdraw cash at Incheon Airport?

Yes. Incheon International Airport lists multiple ATM facilities from Korean banks across Terminals 1 and 2, including some facilities with 24-hour operation. Individual card compatibility still depends on the ATM and your card network.
